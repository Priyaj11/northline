## What this is, and what it is not

QA engineer acting as proxy for a business user. This is NOT business acceptance testing in the strict sense, and the distinction is recorded rather than glossed over: acceptance testing answers "is this the right thing", which only somebody who owns the business outcome can answer. A single-engineer project has no such person. What these scenarios do provide is the other half of acceptance testing: end-to-end journeys written in business language, executed by hand through the interface a customer uses, judged against outcomes a customer would recognise rather than against technical assertions.

### What the statuses mean

    covered by automation      the journey is exercised end to end by
                               automated tests that pass. Not separately
                               walked by hand, and this document says so
                               rather than implying a manual pass.

    designed, not executed     written and reviewable, but nobody has
                               performed it. No result is claimed.

    executed                   performed by hand with results recorded.

Recording 'covered by automation' rather than 'passed' is deliberate.
Automated tests answer whether the parts work. Acceptance testing asks
whether a person can accomplish the goal, and those are different questions
even when the same steps are involved.

## Scenarios

### UAT-01  A new customer can join the bank and start using it

    persona       Someone who has just been given access to online banking
    requirements  REQ-AUTH-005, REQ-AUTH-001, REQ-ACCT-001
    status        covered by automation

**Business outcome**

A person can register, is immediately able to see their money, and can come back later and get in again.

**Steps**

1. Open the bank's home page.
2. Choose to register, and complete the form with your own details.
3. Confirm you can see an account and a balance.
4. Log out, then log back in with the username and password you chose.

**Acceptance criteria**

- You are signed in immediately after registering, without a second step.
- At least one account is visible with a balance.
- The same credentials work again after logging out.

**Automated coverage**

- `tests/ui/test_registration_ui.py::test_a_new_customer_can_register_and_is_signed_in`
- `tests/ui/test_registration_ui.py::test_a_registered_customer_can_log_back_in`

### UAT-02  A customer can see where their money is

    persona       Someone checking their balances before paying for something
    requirements  REQ-ACCT-001, REQ-ACCT-002, REQ-TXN-001
    status        covered by automation

**Business outcome**

A customer can find out how much money they have and what has been happening in an account, without help.

**Steps**

1. Log in as the demo customer.
2. Look at the list of accounts and note one balance.
3. Open that account.
4. Read its recent transactions.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Every account the customer holds appears in the list.
- The balance on the account page matches the one in the list.
- Transactions show a date, a description and an amount.
- It is clear which transactions took money out and which put it in.

**Automated coverage**

- `tests/ui/test_accounts_ui.py::test_the_overview_lists_the_customers_accounts`
- `tests/ui/test_accounts_ui.py::test_every_displayed_balance_matches_the_service`
- `tests/ui/test_billpay_ui.py::test_the_account_activity_lists_transactions`

### UAT-03  A customer can move money between their own accounts

    persona       Someone covering a bill from savings
    requirements  REQ-XFER-001, REQ-XFER-002, REQ-XFER-006
    status        covered by automation

**Business outcome**

Money moves where the customer intended, the amount is right, and they can see afterwards that it happened.

**Steps**

1. Note the balances of two of your accounts.
2. Transfer 100.00 from the first to the second.
3. Read the confirmation.
4. Return to the account list and check both balances.
5. Open each account and find the transaction.

**Acceptance criteria**

- The confirmation names the amount and both accounts.
- The first balance is exactly 100.00 lower.
- The second is exactly 100.00 higher.
- The transfer appears in both accounts' history.

**Automated coverage**

- `tests/ui/test_transfer_ui.py::test_a_valid_transfer_shows_a_confirmation`
- `tests/ui/test_transfer_ui.py::test_a_transfer_through_the_browser_moves_exactly_the_amount`
- `tests/database/test_transaction_correctness.py::test_a_transfer_writes_one_transaction_to_each_account`

### UAT-04  A customer can pay a bill

    persona       Someone paying a utility bill from their current account
    requirements  REQ-BILL-001, REQ-BILL-003
    status        covered by automation

**Business outcome**

### UAT-05  A customer is told when they have made a mistake

    persona       Someone who mistypes an amount
    requirements  REQ-XFER-004, REQ-BILL-002
    status        designed, not executed

**Business outcome**

When a customer enters something the bank cannot accept, they are told clearly enough to fix it themselves.

**Steps**

1. Start a transfer and type letters into the amount.
2. Submit it and read what the page says.
3. Try again leaving the amount empty.
4. Start a bill payment and leave the payee name empty, then submit.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Each attempt produces a message, not silence.
- The message says what is wrong in words a customer would understand.
- No money moves in any of these attempts.

**Automated coverage**

- `tests/ui/test_transfer_ui.py and tests/api/test_transfer_validation_api.py confirm that invalid amounts are refused and move no money`

Partially covered: automation confirms invalid amounts are refused and that no money moves. What is NOT covered, and what this scenario exists for, is whether the wording is intelligible to a customer and whether it is clear which field each message concerns. That is a human judgement and it has not been made. Related evidence does exist from another direction: TC-ACC-007 established that the error text is present and readable but is not programmatically associated with the field, recorded in DEF-003.

### UAT-06  A customer can leave safely on a shared computer

    persona       Someone using online banking on a library machine
    requirements  REQ-AUTH-004, REQ-SEC-004
    status        designed, not executed

**Business outcome**

After logging out, the next person at that computer cannot see the previous customer's money.

**Steps**

1. Log in and open the account overview.
2. Copy the page address from the browser.
3. Log out.
4. Paste the address back in and press enter.
5. Press the browser's back button several times.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Logging out returns you to the login page.
- Pasting the address back shows no account information.
- Going back in history shows no account information.

**Automated coverage**

- `tests/security/test_session_ui.py::test_the_session_ends_when_the_customer_logs_out`
- `tests/security/test_session_ui.py::test_a_protected_page_returns_no_customer_data_without_a_session`

Partially covered: automation confirms that logging out ends access and that a protected page returns no data without a session. What is NOT covered is the browser BACK BUTTON after logout. Browsers cache pages aggressively, and no test in this project exercises history navigation. That gap is real and is stated rather than implied to be covered.
